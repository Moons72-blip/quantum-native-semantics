# A Private-State Partnership Model for Financial Crime Enforcement
## Specialized Investigation and Recovery Under Strict State Authorization

---

## Introduction

Large-scale fraud, shell companies, organized tax evasion, and complex financial schemes cost society enormous amounts of money every year. Governments often lack the specialized capacity required to efficiently investigate and prosecute these highly technical cases.

At the same time, many citizens increasingly feel that:

- major financial offenders often escape meaningful consequences;
- penalties fail to provide real deterrence;
- and financial crime can remain profitable even after legal action.

This proposal explores an alternative enforcement model in which specialized private legal and financial investigation teams operate under strict state authorization to assist in combating large-scale fraud.

---

## Objective

The objective of this model is to:

- significantly increase the detection rate of organized financial fraud;
- rapidly deploy specialized expertise;
- recover public funds more efficiently;
- and make fraud economically unattractive.

### Core principle:

**Fraud should never be more profitable than honest business activity.**

---

## Core Structure of the System

### The state retains:

- full legal authority;
- exclusive licensing powers;
- oversight responsibilities;
- sanctioning authority;
- and final prosecutorial control.

### Licensed private firms may, under strict legal conditions:

- initiate fraud investigations;
- gather evidence;
- expose complex financial structures;
- and prepare legal cases for prosecution.

### Compensation is granted only:

- after proven recovery of public funds;
- following judicial validation;
- and within legally defined limits.

---

## Compensation Model

Participating firms receive a limited percentage of successfully recovered public assets.

### Conditions include:

- no compensation without verified recovery;
- no compensation in cases involving procedural misconduct or abuse of authority;
- full transparency regarding fees and compensation;
- statutory caps on percentage-based rewards.

### The purpose is to:

- encourage efficiency;
- create performance incentives;
- and reduce the initial financial burden on the state.

---

## Strict Oversight Mechanisms

To prevent abuse, the system operates under extensive oversight and accountability measures.

### Possible Safeguards

#### 1. Mandatory Licensing

All participating firms must obtain a state-issued authorization license.

#### 2. Continuous Auditing

Independent oversight bodies conduct regular audits and reviews.

#### 3. Immediate Revocation for Abuse

Licenses are immediately revoked in cases involving:

- conflicts of interest;
- corruption;
- coercive settlement practices;
- manipulation of investigations;
- or abuse of authority.

#### 4. Criminal Liability

Abusive conduct by participating firms may result in:

- severe financial penalties;
- permanent professional disqualification;
- and criminal prosecution.

#### 5. Transparency Requirements

All:

- compensation structures;
- settlements;
- procedures;
- and potential conflicts of interest

must remain publicly reviewable.

---

## Settlements

The current use of limited financial settlements in major fraud cases often weakens deterrence and public trust.

This model therefore proposes that:

- settlements remain exceptional rather than routine;
- full financial restitution is mandatory;
- additional penalties remain proportional to the offense;
- and repeat offenders face automatic escalation toward criminal prosecution.

### Objective:

**to prevent fraud from being treated as a manageable business expense.**

---

## Expected Advantages

### Higher Detection Rates

Specialized external capacity increases the likelihood of uncovering complex fraud.

### Stronger Deterrence

Fraud becomes financially riskier and less attractive.

### Faster Access to Expertise

Complex international financial schemes can be investigated more efficiently.

### Reduced Upfront State Costs

Private participants absorb part of the operational risk.

### Recovery of Public Funds

More lost public resources can be returned to society.

---

## Potential Risks

This model also carries important risks, including:

- excessive legal aggression;
- over-litigation;
- political misuse;
- conflicts of interest;
- lobbying influence;
- and concentration of power among large legal firms.

For this reason, the system can only function responsibly if:

- courts remain independent;
- oversight institutions remain strong;
- media remains free;
- and transparency standards remain extremely high.

---

## Conclusion

This proposal is not a privatization of justice, but an attempt to deploy specialized private capacity in a controlled manner to protect public resources.

### Its central premise is simple:

**As long as fraud remains more profitable than the risks associated with prosecution, financial crime will continue to be economically rational.**

A modern state must therefore possess:

- sufficient detection capacity;
- specialized expertise;
- and an enforcement structure capable of making large-scale financial fraud structurally unprofitable.
