## Abstract

This project proposes a lightweight semantic communication paradigm for quantum computing in which minimal quantum operations—elementary unitary transformations or measurement primitives—function as the smallest meaningful computational units. Rather than translating classical symbolic language into quantum circuits, the framework treats quantum operations themselves as semantic carriers. Meaning emerges from operational structure and transformation effects rather than from external symbolic interpretation.

The model introduces a quantum-native semantic layer capable of supporting scalable hybrid computation, hardware-adaptive communication, and bidirectional interaction between humans, classical systems, and quantum devices. By grounding semantics directly in quantum operations, this approach opens a possible path toward operationally interpretable quantum computation and future quantum-native interfaces.

## Introduction

Quantum computing currently lacks a human-interpretable and hardware-flexible semantic communication layer. Existing approaches generally rely on one of the following paradigms:

- Classical programming languages compiled into quantum circuits
- Category-theoretic or diagrammatic frameworks
- Quantum natural language processing (QNLP)
- Hardware-specific APIs, transpilers, and instruction sets

These methods are powerful and mathematically sophisticated, but they remain fundamentally indirect: meaning originates in classical symbolic systems and is subsequently translated into quantum operations.

This project proposes an alternative framework in which minimal quantum operations themselves act as semantic primitives. Instead of viewing quantum gates as low-level implementation details, the framework treats them as the smallest meaningful units of computation and communication.

**In this model, semantics is operational rather than symbolic. Meaning is encoded directly in transformations applied to quantum states.**

## Core Idea

A minimal quantum operation—such as a single-qubit rotation, entangling transformation, or elementary measurement—can function as a semantic unit within a quantum-native communication system.

The semantic content of an operation arises not from symbolic labels, but from its structural and transformational effects on quantum states.

## Operational Semantics

In this framework, semantic meaning is associated with transformation classes rather than symbolic tokens.

For example:

- **Superposition-generating operations** may represent ambiguity, exploration, or basis transition
- **Entangling operations** may represent dependency, contextual linkage, or relational structure
- **Measurement operations** may represent distinction, collapse, or resolution
- **Phase operations** may encode contextual modification or relational interference

Meaning therefore becomes:

- Context-dependent
- Compositional
- Non-Boolean
- Transformation-based

Concepts may exist in superposition, interfere with one another, or remain partially unresolved until measurement.

This introduces the possibility of a quantum-native semantic geometry rather than a purely symbolic grammar.
